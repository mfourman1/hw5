One agent holding the same MCP tools could have run ticket 103 as: get_ticket → get_ticket_urgency → check_stock + get_vendor_lead_time + two quote_bulk_price calls → draft. I'd expect about 5–6 model requests instead of the 16 the team used.

The team pays for every hand-off. Each agent gets its own prompt and tool list, and each re-reads the ticket: ticket 103 was read 4 times (get_ticket by the Boss, Inventory, Accounting and Customer Service).

Every trust problem was a hand-off problem

Every failure I fixed came from one agent not trusting a fact another agent had already found:

  • Accounting re-asking Facilities about the lease in Problems 7, 8 and on ticket 102.
  • Customer Service re-asking Facilities in Problem 8.
  • Run 1 of #101, where Accounting made Inventory "prove" invoice 501.

A single agent would have the get_lease or get_invoice result in its own context. There would be nothing to re-verify, and no rule patches like "a verdict the Boss passes on counts as confirmed".

What the team is still worth

The split mirrors real shop controls.

  • Separation of duties: Facilities verifies, Accounting proposes, and a person approves.
  • Least privilege: only Accounting can propose payments, and Customer Service can't see costs or margins.
  • A clear audit trail: it records who did what.

Most of the money safety is in the tools anyway: record_payment is human-only, and quote_bulk_price and propose_payment enforce the limits. A single agent would be almost as safe.

My take: a "desk agent" for routine one-department tickets (rent checks, simple inquiries), escalating to the full team only when departments collide. Ticket 101 is that case: stock, a vendor bill and the cash buffer all at once.

4Three new problems this team could solve with the tools I built

1. A rent notice that doesn't match the lease (a scam)

The ticket: an email from "Elm City Property Mgmt" asks for $2,600 by Friday.

  • Facilities' get_lease(1) shows $2,400 to Elm City Properties. Under rule 4 that is a contradiction, so the notice is flagged as a possible scam.
  • Even if an agent tried, propose_payment refuses an amount that doesn't match the lease ("$2,600.00 does not match the $2,400.00 on file").
  • So no proposal ever reaches the approval card, and no money moves.

It is ticket 102's flow, used as a defense.

2. The Yale AI Club counter-offers at 15% off

The ticket: a follow-up to #103 asking for 15% off.

The discount rules are enforced in code, not left to the model's judgment.

5Three new problems this team could not solve, and what it would need

1. Returns and exchanges

The ticket: once the reprint arrives and Tauhid gets his tee, he wants to swap size S for M. Or the AI Club sends back 3 of its hoodies.

Why the team can't do it today:

  • There is no record of what a customer bought or paid. The database has no orders or sales.
  • check_stock can only read inventory, so nothing can put a returned tee back on the shelf.
  • Cash only ever goes out, to bills: the Cash tab is just −$2,400 and −$840. There is no refund.

What it would need:

  • Tables: orders, order_items and returns.
  • MCP tools:
    • get_order;
    • open_return(order_id, sku, size, qty, reason);
    • restock_item(sku, size, qty), an inventory write tool, only for items Inventory marked "resaleable";
    • propose_refund, plus a human-only record_refund that mirrors record_payment.
  • Shop rules: a return window (for example 30 days), "unworn with tags", and no returns on custom or embroidered pieces.
  • Agents: a Returns agent, or a returns lane shared by Customer Service, Inventory and Accounting. Customer Service talks to the customer, Inventory inspects and restocks, Accounting proposes the refund, and a person approves it on the board.

2. Actually filling an order and taking the money

The ticket: the AI Club replies "yes, the 8 we can have now at $52.20".

Why the team can't do it today:

  • It can quote, but it can't sell. Nothing reserves the 8 hoodies: inventory never changed in any of our runs.
  • Nothing records the $417.60 coming in.
  • Nothing books QuickShip CT for campus delivery. get_vendor_lead_time only tells us it takes 1 day.

What it would need:

  • MCP tools:
    • create_order;
    • reserve_stock / ship_order, which decrement inventory and enforce "no ship while the restock invoice is unpaid" in code, not just in the prompt;
    • record_sale, so cash goes up, through a payment processor or a person;
    • book_courier(vendor_id=3).
  • Agents: a Sales/Fulfillment agent, with Inventory gaining a guarded write permission.

3. Planning cash and buying stock before we run out

The situation: after this run, checking is $160.00, $340 below the buffer, and lease 1's next rent ($2,400) is due 2026-10-02 (Cash tab). The AI Club still needs 12 more hoodies.

What the team can do now, and why it still isn't enough:

  • After a final review, Accounting can propose a purchase order (propose_purchase_order) for a person to approve (record_purchase). The scenario expects Accounting to prepare purchase orders, and this closes that gap.
  • For the 12 hoodies, though, it would refuse today: 12 × $22 unit cost = $264, and checking has only $160.
  • The team still can't project how much cash will be on hand by October.
  • It can't say how to cover the $2,240 gap before rent. In this homework cash only goes out; there is no revenue.

What it would need:

  • MCP tools:
    • list_upcoming_bills(days), which reads leases.next_due and open invoices;
    • forecast_cash(days);
    • purchase orders that wait for cash: today a purchase order is refused outright when cash is short. It could instead be queued until the forecast says it's affordable.
  • Agents: a Finance/Purchasing agent that runs weekly. It would warn the Boss when the buffer is at risk, and queue shortfalls like #103's 12 hoodies as purchase orders until cash allows.
